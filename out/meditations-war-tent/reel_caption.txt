The Meditations were written on a war front.

In the 170s AD, Marcus Aurelius spent years in camps along the Danube, fighting the Marcomanni and the Quadi. Cassius Dio says he was frail and barely ate. Every decision in the empire still landed on him.

So he wrote short notes to himself, in Greek. Book 1 of his Meditations ends with a place, not a flourish: "Among the Quadi at the Granua."

His rule for those notes, from Meditations 4.3: "Let thy principles be brief and fundamental."

That's the Marcus Aurelius Meditations habit I'd steal for mental clarity. Before anything big, write 3 lines. What's true. What's mine. What I'll do next.

Try it before your next hard conversation today, then tell me in the comments what your 3 lines were.
